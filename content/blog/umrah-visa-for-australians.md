Australians need a visa to enter Saudi Arabia for Umrah, and there are two main ways to get one: the Saudi tourist eVisa, which Australian passport holders can apply for online and which permits Umrah outside the Hajj season, or a dedicated Umrah visa arranged through the Nusuk platform or a travel agent. Whichever you hold, you also need an Umrah permit booked in the Nusuk app. This guide to the Umrah visa for Australia sets out each route, what it allows and what to check before you apply.

> **Travel advice, 17 November 2026:** the Australian Government's [Smartraveller](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/middle-east/saudi-arabia) currently advises **do not travel** to Saudi Arabia, and says to postpone any planned travel, including travel for religious purposes. It reports that air travel is significantly disrupted, with airspace closures, cancellations and delays. Read the latest advice before you apply for a visa or pay a deposit. Travelling against official advice can leave you without travel insurance, so ask your insurer what it would cover. Visa fees are not refunded once an application is lodged: see our [refund policy](/refund-policy).

## Do Australians need a visa for Umrah?

Yes. Smartraveller is plain about it: you need a valid visa to enter Saudi Arabia, and a visa does not promise entry at the border. It also notes that only Muslims may enter Makkah and Madinah.

The useful part for pilgrims is this line from the same page: all Saudi visa holders of Muslim faith may perform Umrah at any time of year, except around Hajj, when only Hajj visa and permit holders can enter Makkah. So the question is less "which visa is the Umrah visa" and more "which valid Saudi visa suits my trip".

For most Australians that comes down to two choices.

## Umrah visa or tourist eVisa: which one do Australians use?

Both are electronic. Both let you travel between Makkah, Madinah and other Saudi cities. The differences are in who applies, how long the visa lasts and what it is tied to.

| | Tourist eVisa | Dedicated Umrah visa |
|---|---|---|
| Who applies | You, on the official eVisa portal | Arranged through the Nusuk platform or a travel agent |
| Open to Australian passports | Yes, Australia is on the eligible list | Yes |
| Validity | One year, multiple entries | Enter within three months of issue |
| Stay | Up to 90 days in total, cannot be extended | Listed as 90 days, cannot be extended |
| Umrah allowed | Yes, excluding Hajj | Yes, that is its purpose |
| Health cover | A health policy is linked to the visa; Smartraveller says the cover may be limited | Mandatory health insurance comes with the visa |
| Suits | Independent travellers, repeat visitors, land-only bookings | Group packages where the operator handles the paperwork |

Sources: the [official Saudi eVisa portal](https://visa.visitsaudi.com/), the [Ministry of Hajj and Umrah FAQ](https://www.haj.gov.sa/en/Faqs) and Smartraveller, all read on 17 November 2026.

If you hold another kind of Saudi visa, such as a family visit visa, the Smartraveller line above applies to you as well. If you are only passing through a Saudi airport, Smartraveller says you may need a transit visa and should check with your airline.

## How do you apply for the Saudi eVisa from Australia?

The official portal is [visa.visitsaudi.com](https://visa.visitsaudi.com/). Smartraveller warns travellers to be cautious of fraudulent visa links, so type the address yourself instead of following an ad. The portal describes three steps:

1. Fill in the application online.
2. Pay the visa fee.
3. Get your visa online.

A few things worth knowing before you start:

- **The fee.** The portal shows the fee at the payment step. Published figures on other websites disagree with each other, so we have not quoted one here. Trust only what the official portal charges you.
- **Children.** Smartraveller notes that tourists under 18 may not be allowed to enter unless accompanied by a guardian.
- **Work.** A tourist visa cannot be used for any form of work.
- **Overstaying.** Smartraveller lists a significant fine, detention while you wait to be deported, and a ban on returning. You cannot leave until the fine is paid.
- **Dual nationals.** If you travel with two passports, you can only leave Saudi Arabia on the passport you entered with. Saudi Arabia does not recognise dual nationality for its own citizens, so Australians who also hold Saudi citizenship should read the Smartraveller section on this before applying.

## What does the dedicated Umrah visa allow?

The Ministry of Hajj and Umrah describes the Umrah visa as an electronic visa for pilgrims that allows freedom of movement between Makkah, Madinah and all cities of the Kingdom during the stay. On its FAQ page, last updated 15 November 2026, the Ministry says:

- the fee for arrivals from abroad is estimated at 300 Saudi riyals
- the stay is 90 days and cannot be extended
- the visa cannot be extended into the Hajj season
- you must enter Saudi Arabia within three months of the visa being issued

One answer on that page also says the stay must not exceed 30 days. The two lines do not agree, so treat the dates printed on your own visa as the rule, and ask whoever arranged it to confirm them before you lock in flights.

Smartraveller describes Nusuk as the platform that can handle registration and visa processing for Umrah pilgrims. In practice, pilgrims travelling in a group rarely apply themselves. The operator lodges the application with the package details, and the electronic visa is issued to you.

## Do you still need a Nusuk permit once you have a visa?

Yes. The visa gets you into the country. The permit gets you into the ritual.

The Ministry says a permit is required to perform Umrah and to pray in the Rawdah in the Prophet's Mosque in Madinah, and that both are booked through the Nusuk or Tawakkalna apps. Permits are checked in the app on arrival. Praying in the rest of the Prophet's Mosque needs no booking.

Two practical points from our groups. Set up the Nusuk app and log in before you leave Australia, while you still have your usual phone number and a steady connection. And book the Rawdah as soon as your dates are fixed, because you cannot edit a Rawdah time: the Ministry says you cancel it in the app and book again.

## What else do Australians need before flying?

**Passport.** Smartraveller reminds travellers that many countries require at least six months of validity, including for transit. Check the expiry date now, not the week before the visa application.

**Meningococcal vaccination.** The Saudi Ministry of Health requires everyone performing Umrah, from every country, to be vaccinated against meningococcal disease. Its [health requirements for Umrah](https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/HealthAwareness/Pilgrims-Health/Documents/Health-Regulations-Umrah-EN.pdf) ask for a quadrivalent ACYW conjugate vaccine received within the last five years and at least ten days before arrival. The latest edition online is for 1447H (2026), so check the health guidelines on the Nusuk Umrah portal close to your travel date. See your GP early and keep the certificate with your passport.

**COVID-19.** The same document requires proof of vaccination or immunity only for certain groups, including people over 65, pregnant women and people with listed chronic conditions.

**Insurance.** The health cover attached to a Saudi visa is not travel insurance. Smartraveller says to check the health policy linked to an eVisa because its cover may be limited. For a Level 4 destination it also says most standard travel insurance policies will not cover you. Get your insurer's answer in writing.

## Can women and children get a visa for Umrah?

The Ministry's FAQ answers "yes, it is allowed" to whether a woman may come for Umrah without a mahram. That is the Saudi entry rule. Whether you should travel that way is a religious question, and schools of thought differ, so the group's Shaykh will advise you personally.

For children, the Ministry gives five as the minimum age for an Umrah visa or an Umrah permit. Families with younger children should talk to us before paying for anything, because how this is applied can differ between visa types.

## When can you not perform Umrah on these visas?

Around Hajj. Smartraveller says that only Hajj visa and permit holders can enter Makkah at that time, and that access to the airports in Jeddah, Madinah, Yanbu and Taif may be limited to travellers with a valid Hajj visa. The Ministry also says an Umrah visa cannot be extended into the Hajj season. Hajj needs its own visa and is not covered by a tourist eVisa.

The Ministry sets the closing dates for each Umrah season. If you are planning late in the season, ask for the current dates before you book flights.
